## Basics

`passwordPrompt` shows a password form when a document is encrypted, and asks again after a wrong password.

<Demo id="features/password-protection/demo-basics" title="Ask for the password (it is “demo”)" />

The form is labeled, announces an incorrect password as an alert, and marks the field invalid. Without `passwordPrompt`, an encrypted document reports a `PASSWORD_REQUIRED` [error](/react-pdf-viewer/error-states/).

### A known password

Pass `password` when your app already has it; changing it reloads the document.

<Demo id="features/password-protection/demo-known-password" title="Open with a known password" />

## Customization

`renderPasswordPrompt({ incorrect, submit })` replaces the form with your own.

<Demo id="features/password-protection/demo-customization" title="A custom unlock form" />

Default texts are `labels.passwordPrompt`, `passwordLabel`, `passwordSubmit` and `passwordIncorrect`; see [Labels and locale](/react-pdf-viewer/labels/) and the [customization guide](/react-pdf-viewer/customization/).

## Advanced / escape hatch

With [`usePdfDocument`](/react-pdf-viewer/headless/), pass `password` in the options and handle `error.code === 'PASSWORD_REQUIRED'` or `'INCORRECT_PASSWORD'` in your own UI.

## Limitations

- **The password is held in memory.** Anything that controls `password` (props, state) can read it. Workaround: clear it from your state after the document opens if that matters in your threat model.
- **Owner restrictions are not enforced.** Permissions such as “no printing” or “no copying” set by the PDF author are not applied by the viewer. Workaround: hide `print` and `download` from the toolbar and leave `textLayer` off for such documents.
